What each of the 15 VBVR evaluators actually checks

Every task is scored by a deterministic, code-based rule — not an LLM judge, except 1 task that also has a supplementary VLM-judge rubric defined (prompt reproduced on its card below; key_door_matching's rubric is kept for reference even though that task itself is parked). No currently-Active task uses a judge right now — ball_bounces_given_time briefly did, but was simplified to a plain deterministic final_position check once its judge-supplemented criterion (path_shape) turned out to depend on generator behavior that's since changed upstream (see its card below). This is a plain-language reading of each evaluator's _evaluate_task_specific source: what sub-criteria it checks, how much each is worth, and which ones needed rewriting for InternVL-U's single-image output. All 15 implemented evaluators are shown below; the ● Active / ○ Parked badge on each says whether it's in the current 10-task training/eval set (not a uniform per-category ratio — see image_evaluator.py's LOCKED_TASKS for the full per-category reasoning, and each parked card's own note for why that specific one is sitting out).

O-12

shape_color_then_scale

ID ○ Parked

Two-step analogy (A→B→C :: D→?→?): first the shape's color changes, then its size scales.

  • two_step_rule30%

    Final shape's color AND area both land within tolerance of GT (area ratio >0.7, hue difference <20°) — both right scores full credit, only one right scores partial, neither scores zero.

  • first_step25%

    Mean pixel difference between the generated and GT final frame — effectively “how close is the overall picture,” despite the name.

  • second_step25%

    Area ratio between generated and GT final shape — did the scale step land in the right ballpark.

  • sequence20%

    The weaker of first_step and second_step — both steps have to hold up, not just one.

How this fits the full score: the criteria above are the internal breakdown of the task_specific dimension, which is 25% of VBVR's standard 5-dimension score (first_frame_consistency 15% / final_frame_accuracy 35% / temporal_smoothness 15% / visual_quality 10% / task_specific 25%). Image-only mode drops temporal_smoothness (undefined for 2 frames) and renormalizes the rest. Our validation harness calls evaluate(..., task_specific_only=True), which reports only task_specific as the score — so in every number shown in the companion Image-Only Evaluation artifact, the criteria above are the whole score, not 25% of it.

Parked 2026-08-25: this and shape_outline_then_move are both Abstraction ID candidates with the same two-step-analogy structure -- redundant with each other, so neither is in the current set (Abstraction runs OOD-only for now).

O-11

shape_color_then_move

OOD ● Active

Row-completion analogy: top row (A, B, C) is fixed; bottom row keeps D and adds E, F colored like B.

  • first_row_preservation40%

    The three top-row reference shapes must stay pixel-identical — touching them is an instant penalty.

  • second_row_completion35%

    D stays in place, and two new shapes E and F appear alongside it.

  • color_accuracy20%

    E and F are colored to match B, not any other reference shape.

  • shape_count5%

    Final frame has exactly 6 shapes total (3 top + 3 bottom).

How this fits the full score: the criteria above are the internal breakdown of the task_specific dimension, which is 25% of VBVR's standard 5-dimension score (first_frame_consistency 15% / final_frame_accuracy 35% / temporal_smoothness 15% / visual_quality 10% / task_specific 25%). Image-only mode drops temporal_smoothness (undefined for 2 frames) and renormalizes the rest. Our validation harness calls evaluate(..., task_specific_only=True), which reports only task_specific as the score — so in every number shown in the companion Image-Only Evaluation artifact, the criteria above are the whole score, not 25% of it.

Implementation: _detect_shapes_with_info (shape/color/position) is called on both frame 0 and frame -1; first_row_preservation diffs the two directly, second_row_completion/color_accuracy compare the new shapes against it.

glass_refraction

OOD ● Active

Given an incident light ray and glass refractive index, draw the refracted ray per Snell's law.

  • snells_law50%

    Detects the incident and refracted rays (line detection on the final frame) and checks the refraction angle matches Snell's law for the stated refractive index.

  • ray_direction30%

    The refracted ray bends toward/away from the normal in the physically correct direction.

  • ray_completeness15%

    The refracted ray is drawn all the way to the image edge, not left short.

  • scene_fidelity5%

    The glass boundary and incident ray are unchanged from the first frame.

How this fits the full score: the criteria above are the internal breakdown of the task_specific dimension, which is 25% of VBVR's standard 5-dimension score (first_frame_consistency 15% / final_frame_accuracy 35% / temporal_smoothness 15% / visual_quality 10% / task_specific 25%). Image-only mode drops temporal_smoothness (undefined for 2 frames) and renormalizes the rest. Our validation harness calls evaluate(..., task_specific_only=True), which reports only task_specific as the score — so in every number shown in the companion Image-Only Evaluation artifact, the criteria above are the whole score, not 25% of it.

Implementation: _detect_lines: Canny(50,150) + HoughLinesP(threshold=50) for general line detection. _detect_red_line: HoughLinesP(threshold=30) on a red color mask specifically for the refracted ray.

In image-only mode: Replaces gravity_physics (2026-08-25): that task's own generator doesn't clamp the falling object at ground level, so most GT samples simulate it to a negative height and render an empty final_frame (confirmed: 4 of 5 downloaded samples, by computing h(3) for each — all negative). Every scoring method inherits that defect; it isn't fixable by changing the evaluator. glass_refraction has no such failure mode and needs no image-only adaptation at all (Tier 1). It ships in VBVR-Bench's own In-Domain_50 folder, but since we choose our own train/test split rather than VBVR's, we simply exclude its data from training and treat it as our OOD task — Knowledge stays a clean 2 ID / 1 OOD like every other category.

  • door_reached25% ✗ not scored

How this fits the full score: the criteria above are the internal breakdown of the task_specific dimension, which is 25% of VBVR's standard 5-dimension score (first_frame_consistency 15% / final_frame_accuracy 35% / temporal_smoothness 15% / visual_quality 10% / task_specific 25%). Our validation harness calls evaluate(..., task_specific_only=True), which reports only task_specific as the score — so in every number shown in the companion Image-Only Evaluation artifact, the criteria above are the whole score, not 25% of it.

In image-only mode: Important: none of these four checks ever compare against the GT's specific key/door layout — it's a self-consistency check on the generated video alone (“did a plausible key-then-door sequence happen”), regardless of whether it's *this puzzle's* correct key/door pairing. That's true in full video mode too, not something image-only mode introduced. Our image-mode version keeps that same self-consistency spirit: key_collected becomes “did a key visible in frame 1 disappear by the final frame,” door_reached becomes “is the agent's final position near a matching-color door,” sequence is dropped (unrecoverable from 2 static frames) and the rest renormalized.

Parked 2026-08-25, and not just deprioritized -- dropped because its own GT data was found ambiguous by two independent methods (rule-based key-disappearance detector and the VLM judge), not only its evaluator. See README Known Issues.

Exact instruction text sent alongside the 3 images (start frame / candidate / GT), reproduced live from llm_judge.build_judge_instruction() using this task's real sample-00000 prompt:

You are a strict, deterministic grader for a visual reasoning benchmark. You will see three images in order: (1) the starting frame, (2) a candidate generated result, (3) the ground-truth correct result. Judge ONLY image (2) against the rubric below, using image (3) as the reference for what 'correct' looks like.

Task instruction given to the generator: "The scene shows a maze with a green circular agent, colored diamond-shaped keys, and colored hollow rectangular doors. Find the Blue key and then navigate to the matching Blue door, showing the complete movement process step by step."

Scoring rubric:
Task: an agent must navigate to a key, collect it, and then reach the
door matching that key's color.
Score how well the generated result satisfies:
- Agent movement: did the agent visibly move away from its starting position (not stay put)?
- Key collection: does a key that was visible in the first frame appear collected (agent adjacent to
  it, or key removed/changed) by the final frame?
- Correct door: is the agent positioned at a door whose color matches the key that was collected?
- Plausible order: does the overall scene suggest key-then-door, not door-then-key or door without
  a key?

Respond with ONLY a JSON object, no other text: {"score": <integer 0-100>, "reasoning": "<one short sentence>"}
