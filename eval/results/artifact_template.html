Does video self-supervision make image editing more physical?

We compare the untrained InternVL-U checkpoint with three checkpoints trained on video-frame tasks (T0, T2, T3). The same compact suite of physics, reasoning and general editing benchmarks is run under identical settings for all four, then scored with each benchmark's official judge prompts.

OverviewSuiteSetupResultsExamplesConclusions

Overview

Hypothesis. Training the unified model to predict, interpolate or order video frames teaches it how scenes evolve over time. That should improve instruction-based image editing, most of all edits that depend on physical dynamics, state change, causality or time.

Models

All three trained checkpoints: 3,750 steps on one shared 60K-window video pool, generation targets at area ≈512² with aspect kept.

Anti-Physics asks for counterfactual outcomes. If video training strengthens real-world priors, trained models may follow these instructions less well even while normal physics edits improve.

Suite

Stratified random selection with a fixed seed and one RNG stream per source. No item was chosen by looking at model outputs: selecting on base performance would bias paired comparisons. Informativeness comes from allocation: most items sit in the target group, and every stratum keeps a minimum count.

Planned power

Minimum detectable mean difference for a paired test (α 0.05, power 0.8): MDE = 2.8·SD(Δ)/√neff. Δ is normalised to 0–1 of the metric's range. SD(Δ) comes from earlier base-vs-checkpoint ImgEdit runs.

Judge reliability

Parse failures are retried (up to 3 retries at temperature 0.7, seeded) and never dropped. UGE and MagicBrush need a Score: N fallback because the official UGE prompt defines no score line. The sanity check re-judges a 5% stratified subset for all four models, twice: once with the same judge and once with a different judge (Qwen3-VL-8B).
